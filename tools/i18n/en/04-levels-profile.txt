A big stone barn. Stores everything and explains nothing. If something's missing, you didn't bring it — it didn't lose it.
A storehouse with an annex: more room, exactly as much order as before. Up to 8 haulers fit inside, their bosses stand outside looking important.
A school with a bell tower. Teaches for 1 gold — record-cheap education, no scholarships, no knowledge beyond what's needed. A graduate can do one thing, but does it firmly.
Second floor, second bell. Teaching twice as fast: honor students pass the exam while the slacker is still looking for his desk. Diplomas are still handed out at the exit, unread.
Chicken, sausage, bread and beer — a menu from a chef who can't cook anything else and is proud of it. A drunk may fall asleep right on the road, and that's no bug, it's tradition.
A tavern with an attic: croissants, steaks, dark beer and candles on the tables. Same prices, bigger ambitions, same level of conversation.
Trash from the bins is brought here. Burning, it makes ash — farms fertilize their fields with it. The only place in the village where junk turns into something useful instead of the other way around.
A bonfire with a stone rim: eats trash twice as fast and brags about it all over the neighbourhood. Still nobody asks to warm up by it.
The herder tames wild goats. A tame goat hauls loads on its own, no union, but with attitude: what it can't carry, it eats.
A pen with a hayloft: more goats, and they eat less hay because they steal the neighbour's. The herder pretends it's training.
The stableman raises horses. A horse with a cart is a carter: hauls a heap of cargo at once and never complains, because there's nobody to complain to.
A stable with a loft: the horses are happier, the stableman sleeps on hay and calls it an apartment. He charges himself rent.
The woodcutter's hut. Fells trees, plants new ones — environmentalists weep, but quietly, because the woodcutter has an axe.
The woodcutter bought a second saw and twice the confidence: twice the logs at a time, the same number of fingers. For now.
Saws logs into planks. Sawdust is food too, if you really have to, and the carpenter has checked.
A sawmill with a mighty wheel: saws a quarter faster and screams louder. The neighbours think it's the same thing.
Pulls stone out of the ground. The ground is usually against it, but nobody thought to ask.
A quarry with a crane: twice the stone at a time, the stonecutter's back stays put. For now.
Mines gold ore. The veins promise everything, the gold comes later, the miner believes. Three in one boat.
A mine with minecarts: digs faster. There's no more gold, but the miners got more important and now carry a candle like a medal.
Smelts ore into gold, burning logs. A pity about the forest, but it sparkles. The village picks sparkly.
A smelter with two furnaces: faster, hotter, and the gnomes smell of singed beard again. That's their perfume.
A farm: wheat and hay. The farmer looks at the sky, the sky looks at the farmer, both waiting for the other to blink.
A farm with a big barn: twice as much fits, and the harvest jokes got twice as long. The harvest didn't.
A hop yard. The hop-grower claims he works for bread. No bread has ever come from hops, and everyone knows it.
A hop yard with a tall drying tower: more cones, and the whole village knows what for. The hop-grower still says "for the aroma".
Brews beer from grain and hops. The only business whose waste is pleasant to drink — so says the brewer, and nobody sober argues.
A brewery with a cellar: also brews dark beer — twice as filling and twice as dangerous for the head. The cellar exists so there's somewhere to look for it afterwards.
The mill grinds grain into flour. The wind loves it and takes no pay — the only worker with no salary and no complaints.
A mill with new sails: grinds faster, and the miller waves at it more often. The sails don't answer, but he doesn't lose hope.
Bakes bread from flour, though he'd rather make buns. The baker's dream — the only thing in the village that rises without yeast.
A bakery with an attic: the bread stayed, but croissants appeared. The baker now says "croissant" breathily and raises an eyebrow as if he invented it.
A pigsty. Pigs grow on hay, gnomes on the pigs' hopes. The pigs' hopes aren't counted.
A pigsty with troughs: holds more pigs, who still don't know what awaits them. They're fine, let them enjoy it.
A pig becomes sausage and a hide. A butcher becomes a philosopher: every evening he ponders mortality, every morning he picks up the cleaver.
A slaughterhouse with a smokehouse: six steaks from one pig. The pig is displeased, the tavern delighted, the butcher wrote the recipe on his apron.
Cures hides into leather. The smell is a free add-on you can't uninstall.
A tannery with big vats: faster, and now the smell reaches the next village. The neighbours consider it a declaration of war.
Sews leather armor for a future army nobody has seen. The armor everyone has seen — it gathers dust in the storehouse.
A workshop with an anvil: sews and rivets faster, yet the army is still "in the works". The works are stored next to the armor.
Mines magic stones. The stones glow — the miner pretends not to be afraid, the stones pretend not to glow. Deal.
A mine with crystal supports: more stones, more light, common sense as before — which is to say, bare minimum.
Stones give their power to mana jars. Works on its own, as proper magic should: no worker, no salary, no thanks.
A circle with a runic rim: two jars of mana per stone now. Alchemists are shocked, the sorcerer is in profit, the stones are in jars.
Mines iron ore. The cliff takes it as a personal insult and rusts in response, but the miner is stubborn: he has a minecart and no vacation soon.
A mine with rails and a winch: twice the ore per trip, and the cliff rusts even more resentfully. The miner got a hard hat and considers himself an engineer.
A bloomery with bellows: logs in, ingots out, soot everywhere. The smelter claims it's by design.
A bloomery with double blast: the new recipe squeezes more ingots from ore for the same log. Soot didn't decrease, but now it's efficient.
The smith bends bows, forges swords and sharpens daggers. Few customers: everyone's an equal and a little afraid of each other.
An armory with a big forge: swords come in pairs, customers not so often. The smith hung a "No discounts" sign and was surprised it worked.
A stone hall with a tower and a yard where they beat dummies. Dummies don't complain, which is why they make the best recruits.
The hall is extended: six fighters fit in the guild, and the skill ceiling is raised to level 14. Training is livelier — the dummies beg for mercy.
A forest lodge with a tower. Archers hit everything except what they aimed at, but they try.
The lodge added a second tier to the range: six archers, 14 levels and very tired targets. The wind is, as before, to blame for everything.
A lopsided house with a cellar. The Gang Boss swears it's all honest, and your weapon he forgot to put back yesterday.
The cellar is extended and smells of secret passages: six sneaks, 14 levels and brisk training. The neighbours' purses tensed in advance.
A tower with a starry dome. Mages are serious until you ask for a trick: then they're doubly serious, because the trick failed.
The tower grew: six mages, 14 levels and studying twice as fast. The stars on the dome now wink, but that's most likely a draft.
The sorcerer makes staves and robes. Magic on installments: mana today, a staff tomorrow, the day after — an explanation why it doesn't spark.
A workshop with an observatory turret: faster and starrier. The sorcerer is inspired; inspiration is a resource too, but the storehouse won't take it.
